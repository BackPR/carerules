# Prickly Pear Meadow Learning Center Parent Handbook

Fictional handbook written to test CareRules.

## Welcome

Welcome to Prickly Pear Meadow Learning Center! We are a licensed Texas child-care center for children from 6 weeks through 5 years old. We believe children learn best through play and warm relationships.

Prickly Pear Meadow Learning Center, 4410 Fictional Mesquite Lane, Texas. Phone: (512) 555-0147.

## Hours of Operation

We are open Monday through Friday, 6:30 a.m. to 6:30 p.m., twelve months a year. We close for New Year's Day, Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving Day and the day after, Christmas Eve, and Christmas Day.

## Enrollment

Enrollment begins with a tour and a conversation with the director. On or before your child's first day, you will sign our enrollment agreement, which includes these policies, and complete the admission form. We also need:

- A statement of your child's health from a health-care professional;
- Your child's immunization record or a valid exemption, plus tuberculosis screening if our local health authority requires it;
- Vision and hearing screening results for children who are 4 years old by September 1, as Texas law requires. You may instead give us a signed, dated statement that the results are on file at your child's pre-K or school (with its name, address, and phone number), or an affidavit of religious exemption.

When we change a policy, we will tell you in writing at least two weeks before the change takes effect and ask you to sign and date the updated copy for your child's file.

## Fees

Weekly tuition is due each Monday by bank draft or card in our ParentLink app. A $75 registration fee is due at enrollment. Payments more than five days late carry a $25 fee, and late pick-up costs $1 per minute after 6:30 p.m.

## Arrival and Pick-Up

Please walk your child to the classroom and sign in and out on the front-desk tablet, which records the time and the adult dropping off or picking up.

We release children only to a parent or to an adult the parent has listed in writing on the authorized pick-up list. We will ask for photo ID from anyone we do not know. If a court order limits a parent's access to a child, please give us a copy; we will follow it immediately and keep it in your child's file.

## Classrooms and Staffing

Children are grouped by age: Infants (6 weeks to 11 months), Young Toddlers (12 to 17 months), Toddlers (18 to 23 months), Twos, Threes, and Pre-K (4s and 5s). We follow the Texas child-to-caregiver ratios and group sizes at all times, based on the age of the children in the group:

| Age of children | Children per caregiver | Maximum group size |
|---|---|---|
| 0–11 months | 4 | 10 |
| 12–17 months | 5 | 13 |
| 18–23 months | 9 | 18 |
| 2 years | 11 | 22 |
| 3 years | 15 | 30 |
| 4 years | 18 | 35 |

Each infant teacher cares for up to six babies, and our infant room holds up to twelve babies when we are full.

- 6:30 Arrival, health check, and quiet centers
- 7:30 Breakfast
- 8:15 Morning circle

- 9:30 Morning snack

- 5:30 Late snack and free play until pick-up

Infant and toddler rooms follow each child's own rhythm for eating and sleeping.

## Physical Activity and Outdoor Play

Active play builds strong muscles, healthy hearts, and confidence, and helps children sleep and learn better. We offer at least two outdoor play times every day, weather permitting. Children 13 months and older spend at least 60 minutes outdoors daily, and infants go outside as long as they enjoy it. Toddlers get at least 60 minutes and Pre-K children at least 90 minutes of moderate to vigorous active play each day, including free play on the playground and in our movement room and teacher-led games like obstacle courses and dancing.

Please send comfortable clothes that can get messy and closed-toe shoes; no flip-flops on the playground. We keep children inside when the heat index is 100°F or above, the wind chill is 32°F or below, there is lightning, or air quality is in the red range. On those days active play moves to our indoor movement room so children still get their full activity time.

## Rest Time

Children 18 months and older rest after lunch on their own labeled cot for up to two and a half hours. Children do not have to sleep; those still awake after an hour get quiet books or puzzles.



## Guidance and Discipline

Discipline at Prickly Pear Meadow is individualized, consistent, matched to each child's understanding, and aimed at teaching self-control. Teachers praise good choices, remind children of expectations with clear, positive words, and redirect them to a better activity. When a child needs to calm down, a teacher may use a brief supervised time away from the group, never longer than one minute per year of the child's age.

We never use spanking or any physical punishment, threats, shaming, yelling, or harsh language. We never grab or pull a child, put anything in a child's mouth, place a child in a locked or dark room or a restrictive device, or require a child to sit silently for long periods. Children who refuse to help clean up may miss that afternoon's snack. Outdoor play is never taken away as a consequence.

If challenging behavior continues, we will meet with you to make a written plan together. We suspend or dismiss a child only as a last resort, when behavior seriously endangers others after that plan has been tried, and we give at least two weeks' written notice except in an emergency.

## Meals and Food Allergies

We serve breakfast, lunch, and snacks that follow the USDA Child and Adult Care Food Program meal patterns. Weekly menus are posted outside each classroom. Water is always available, and we never use food as a reward.

If your child has a food allergy diagnosed by a health-care professional, we need a food allergy emergency plan signed and dated by your child's health-care professional and by you before your child's first day. It lists the foods to avoid, the warning signs, and the steps to take. We keep copies in your child's file and in the classroom. We do not stock unassigned epinephrine auto-injectors; please provide any that are prescribed.

Mothers are welcome to breastfeed here. We have a comfortable adult-size chair in the infant room, and you have the right to breastfeed or provide breast milk for your child while in our care.

## Illness

Teachers do a quick health check as each child arrives. We are not licensed for get-well care, so children must stay home, or will be sent home, when:

- The illness keeps your child from participating comfortably in activities, including outdoor play;
- Your child needs more care than teachers can give without compromising the other children;
- Your child has a fever (above 101°F by mouth, or above 100°F by ear, armpit, or forehead) along with behavior changes or other signs of illness;
- Your child shows signs of possible severe illness, such as lethargy, abnormal breathing, uncontrolled diarrhea, two or more vomiting episodes in 24 hours, rash with fever, or mouth sores with drooling;
- Your child has a diagnosed communicable disease and no medical note saying your child is no longer contagious.

We also follow the Texas school exclusion rules for communicable diseases. Please pick up an ill child within one hour of our call.

## Medication

Before we give any medicine, simply tell your child's teacher at drop-off what to give and when; your verbal okay covers the full course of treatment. Authorizations expire one year after you sign them. Medicine must be in its original container, labeled with your child's full name and the date you brought it in. We give it only as the label or your child's health-care professional directs, never after its expiration date, and record the medicine, date, time, amount, and the staff member who gave it. Medicines are stored out of children's reach.



## Injuries, Medical Emergencies, and Notifying Parents

For minor injuries, staff give first aid and tell you about it at pick-up. If a child needs immediate medical treatment, we will call 911, give first aid or CPR, call you, contact your child's physician if needed, and keep the other children supervised.

We will notify you immediately if your child is hurt and needs medical treatment, becomes ill and needs to be hospitalized, is involved in any situation that placed or may have placed your child at risk, or if a fire, flood, or storm damage makes the center unsafe. We will give you written notice within 48 hours if a child or staff member has a reportable communicable disease, or if lice appear in your child's group.

## Emergency Preparedness

Our written emergency preparedness plan covers evacuation, relocation, and sheltering or lockdown. In an evacuation, teachers move children to the meeting spot at the far end of our parking lot; if we must leave the area, we relocate to Fictional Grace Community Hall, 200 Pecan Way. Infants ride in evacuation cribs, and a named teacher helps each child who needs extra assistance. Teachers bring attendance lists, emergency contacts, and medical consents and count children at every step. We will reach you by ParentLink, text, and phone. Evacuation diagrams are posted in every classroom.

We practice a fire drill every month and are able to get all children outside within three minutes. We practice a severe-weather drill and a lockdown drill at least four times each year, and record every drill.

## Field Trips, Transportation, Water Play, and Animals

We do not provide transportation, swimming, or water play activities, and we do not keep pets or animals at the center. Older children take occasional walking field trips to the library or the park, with your signed permission. We post a notice at the front desk on the morning of each field trip, showing where we are going, when we will leave, and when we expect to return. Children wear Prickly Pear shirts with our name and phone number, and teachers wear bright vests and carry a phone, a first-aid kit, allergy plans and medications, and emergency forms. At least one CPR-trained teacher goes with each group.

## Preventing and Reporting Abuse and Neglect

Every staff member completes child abuse and neglect training each year, including warning signs that a child may be a victim, factors that put children at risk, and prevention techniques. We share this information with families through a spring parent workshop and handouts, and we work with the local children's advocacy center for training and referrals.

Every staff member who suspects abuse, neglect, or exploitation must report it to the director, who will decide whether to call the Texas Abuse and Neglect Hotline. We will notify you immediately if your child is alleged to have been abused or neglected while in our care. If you believe your child has been harmed, call the Texas Abuse Hotline at 1-800-252-5400 or report at www.txabusehotline.org, and call 911 if your child is in danger.

## Children's Records

Children's records are immediately available to your child's caregivers during all hours of operation in case of emergency. You may review your child's record during a conference with your child's teacher or the director.

## Your Rights as a Parent

As a parent, you have the right to:

- Visit the center any time we are open, without advance notice, to observe your child, activities, the building, the grounds, and equipment;
- File a complaint about the center with Texas Child Care Regulation;
- Review our publicly accessible records and your child's records;
- Receive our HHSC inspection reports, posted in the lobby, and instructions for finding our compliance history online through Search Texas Child Care;
- Have us follow a valid court order that prevents another parent from visiting or removing your child;
- Receive contact information for Child Care Regulation, which is posted by our front door;
- View available video of an alleged incident of abuse or neglect involving your child, after we notify the parents of other children shown;
- Receive a copy of our policies;
- Review staff training records and our training curriculum on request;
- Be free from any retaliation for using these rights.

You can read the Texas minimum standards for child-care centers, find the Texas Abuse and Neglect Hotline, and learn about child care regulation on the HHSC website at hhs.texas.gov. Texas law makes any area within 1,000 feet of a child-care center a gang-free zone, where gang-related crimes carry harsher penalties.



## Getting Involved

Families are always welcome. Join our quarterly Family Advisory Group, read to your child's class, help with our spring garden day, or come to fall and spring parent conferences.

## More Health and Safety Policies

**Insurance.** We carry liability insurance of $300,000 per occurrence, as Texas requires. If that ever changes, we will tell every family in writing within 14 days.

**Staff vaccinations.** Employees who work with children must be vaccinated against flu and pertussis, with exemptions for medical reasons or reasons of conscience. Exempt employees wear masks and follow extra hand-washing steps, and we keep a confidential record of each employee's vaccination or exemption.

**Children with special needs.** We welcome children with special care needs and meet with you, and with your permission your child's therapists, to adapt activities, routines, and equipment so your child can take part fully.
