# Marigold Canyon Children's Center: Family Handbook

Fictional handbook written to test CareRules.

## Welcome

Welcome to Marigold Canyon! We are a licensed child care center for infants and preschool children.

- **Director:** Teresa Villanueva-Holt
- **Address:** 1840 Arroyo Linda Drive, Fresno, CA 93710
- **Phone:** (559) 555-0173

Our purpose is to give young children a safe, loving place to grow. Children learn best through play, so teachers plan hands-on activities in art, building, books, music, and science, and use daily routines to build language and independence. Our written plan of operation, which describes our program goals, admission policies, staffing, sample menus, transportation, and rates, is kept on file here, and you may read it at the front desk.

## Admission Policies

These policies are available to anyone who asks and match the limits on our license.

- **Ages:** We accept infants from 6 weeks through 23 months and preschool children from 2 years until they start kindergarten.
- **Who we can serve:** We admit children whose needs can be met by our program and services. We welcome children with disabilities and make reasonable changes so we can meet each child's individual needs.
- **Program and services:** Play-based learning, meals, and nap time, plus an optional weekly music class and developmental screenings.
- **Field trips and transportation:** We take a few walking trips and van trips each year. We do not provide daily transportation to or from home or school.
- **Food:** We provide breakfast, lunch, and two snacks.
- **Medical assessment:** Every child needs a physician's medical assessment, described below.

## Enrolling Your Child

Enrollment begins with a personal interview with the director. We learn about your child's health and development to be sure we can meet your child's needs, and we go over our policies, activities, services, licensing regulations, hours, fees, what happens if your child becomes ill or injured, and our daily health check.

**Admission agreement.** You and the director complete a written admission agreement together. It lists our basic services, optional services and their rates, your basic rate, who pays, the due date and how often payments are made, how the agreement may be changed, refund conditions, the right of Community Care Licensing to inspect our center and records, and the conditions under which the agreement may be ended. We both sign and date it no later than seven calendar days after your child starts, and you receive a copy.

**Medical assessment.** Before your child starts, or within 30 calendar days, we need a written medical assessment by or under the supervision of a licensed physician, no more than one year old. It must include tuberculosis test results, any special needs, prescribed medications, and any contagious disease that would keep your child from care.

**Immunizations.** Before admission, children must have the immunizations California requires for their age, or a valid exemption allowed by law. We keep your child's immunization record on file and update it as new doses are due.

## Tuition, Rates, and Refunds

Our rates are set each year based on the age group and the cost of meeting required staffing. Monthly tuition is $1,980 for infants and $1,450 for preschoolers, billed monthly and due on the 1st. The optional music class is $25 per month. The $100 registration fee is not refundable. We give you at least 30 calendar days' written notice before any rate change.

**Refunds.** If you withdraw with two weeks' written notice, we refund any prepaid tuition beyond the notice period. If we must close for an emergency for more than two business days in a row, we credit the missed days on your next bill. Tuition is not reduced for your child's absences or for holidays.

## Ending Care

Either of us may end the admission agreement with two weeks' written notice. We may end care with shorter notice if tuition is more than 30 days past due, or if, after meeting with you and trying other supports, we cannot safely meet your child's needs.

## Your Rights as a Parent

When your child is accepted, we give you the Notification of Parents' Rights (LIC 995) and the Caregiver Background Check Process form (LIC 995E). We ask you to sign the receipt at the bottom of the LIC 995, which we keep in your child's file. The Parents' Rights poster (PUB 393) is posted by our front door. You have the right to:

- Enter and inspect the center without advance notice whenever we are open. Please show identification and respect the children's routines.
- File a complaint with the local licensing office, and complain or inspect without any discrimination or retaliation.
- Review our public file at the licensing office, and review at our center reports of licensing visits and substantiated complaints from the last three years.
- Ask in writing that a parent not be allowed to visit or take your child, if you show us a certified copy of a court order.
- Receive from us, upon request, the name, address, and telephone number of the local licensing office.
- Be told, upon request, the name and role of any adult at our center who has been granted a criminal record exemption. The licensing office can also give you this information.

## Your Child's Personal Rights

We give you a copy of the Personal Rights form (LIC 613A) and ask you to sign the acknowledgment, which we keep in your child's file. The form is also posted in our lobby. Every child has the right to be treated with dignity, to have safe and comfortable surroundings, to be informed about how to make complaints, to attend religious activities only by your choice, and to receive or refuse medical care as you decide.

## Guidance and Discipline

We guide children with clear limits, redirection, and lots of encouragement. We never use spanking or any physical punishment, and never humiliate, threaten, frighten, or ridicule a child. Children who throw food or leave the table during lunch may lose their afternoon snack that day. Children are never locked in any room or area, and we never place a child in any restraining device. Infants are never kept in a crib, high chair, or playpen as a punishment. None of these practices is allowed even if a parent requests it.

## Hours and Classrooms

We are open Monday through Friday, 6:30 a.m. to 6:00 p.m., year-round, except New Year's Day, Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving and the day after, and Christmas Day. We are licensed for 60 children: 12 infants and 48 preschoolers.

| Room | Ages | Staffing | Children in room |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hummingbird | 6 weeks to 23 months | 1 teacher for every 4 infants | up to 12 |
| Poppy | 2 years | 1 teacher for every 6 children | up to 12 |
| Sunflower | 3 years | 1 teacher and 1 aide for 15 children | up to 15 |
| Redwood | 4 to 5 years | 1 fully qualified teacher and 1 aide with at least 6 early childhood units for 18 children | up to 18 |

A teacher working without an aide may supervise up to 16 preschool children, and teachers always keep children in sight. During nap time, one teacher may watch up to 24 napping preschoolers as long as the other teachers needed for ratio are in the building and immediately available. We provide at least 35 square feet of indoor and 75 square feet of outdoor play space for every child on our license, and one toilet and sink for every 15 children.

## Arrival and Departure

Please bring your child into the classroom and stay until a staff member has done a quick health check and accepted your child. Children with obvious signs of illness, such as fever or vomiting, cannot be accepted. After your child is accepted, sign in on the clipboard by the door using your full legal signature and the time. The adult who picks your child up signs out the same way, and anyone who takes your child out and brings them back during the day signs out and in as well. We release children only to adults you have listed in writing and will ask for photo ID.

## Meals, Menus, and Allergies

Our meals and snacks follow federal Child and Adult Care Food Program meal patterns. Weekly menus are posted on the family board at least one week in advance, and we keep dated copies of menus as served for at least 30 days. We provide modified diets prescribed by your child's physician as a medical necessity, following the doctor's or dietitian's instructions, and we never serve a child any food listed as an allergy in their record. Drinking water is available indoors and outdoors all day.

## Our Infant Program

**Needs and services plan.** Before your baby's first day, the director meets with you to write an infant needs and services plan, which you sign. It includes your baby's feeding plan, the Individual Infant Sleeping Plan (LIC 9227), which you sign and date, any special services your baby needs, and a schedule for future meetings. We update it with you at least every three months.

**Feeding.** Each baby has an individual feeding plan, written with you before the first day, covering schedule, breast milk or formula brand, new foods, textures, and allergies. Bottles must arrive filled and labeled with your baby's name and the date. Bottle-fed babies are fed at least every four hours. Babies are always held for bottle feeding, bottles are never propped, and walking babies do not carry bottles. We never serve honey or corn syrup. Nursing parents are welcome, and we will provide a private space.

**Diapering and toileting.** Babies are diapered on a changing table that is disinfected after every change, and babies are never left alone on it. When your toddler is ready, we write a toilet-training plan with you covering methods, potty equipment, and clothing. Teachers wash their hands with soap and water before every feeding and after every diaper change.

**Sleep.** Babies sleep in their own safety-approved crib with a firm, fitted mattress and nothing else inside except a pacifier with nothing attached: no blankets, pillows, toys, or bumper pads. Babies under 12 months are always placed on their backs to sleep unless a physician has signed a medical exemption. We never swaddle and never cover a baby's head. If a baby falls asleep elsewhere, we move them to their crib as soon as possible. A teacher looks in on sleeping babies about every 30 minutes and writes the time and their initials on the sleep log. Crib sheets are changed daily.

Every baby is in a teacher's direct view at all times. Infant room floors are cleaned and disinfected daily, and we do not use baby walkers.

## Illness, Injuries, and Medication

If your child becomes ill at the center, we move them to our quiet isolation area and call you right away to pick up. We call you immediately for any illness or any injury more serious than a minor cut or scratch. Minor scrapes and bites are cleaned, noted in your child's record, and explained to you at pickup. If we cannot reach you, or if waiting would be dangerous, we will get emergency medical treatment for your child right away.

We give prescription and over-the-counter medicine only with your written approval and instructions for each medicine, matching the label. Medicine must be in its original labeled container with your child's name and the date. All medicine is stored centrally in a locked cabinet or medicine refrigerator. We record every dose and tell you each day when medicine was given.

We report to Community Care Licensing, and also to you, any death, injury needing medical treatment, unusual incident, suspected abuse, epidemic outbreak, poisoning, catastrophe, or fire on the premises.

## Emergency and Disaster Plan

Our written disaster and mass casualty plan is kept at the front desk. The director, or the assistant director when she is away, is in charge, and each staff member has an assigned role. The plan covers fires, floods, and earthquakes, our exit routes, transportation, and supervision of children. If we must evacuate, we walk to Palomitas Community Hall, three blocks east, which is equipped to shelter children until you arrive. Infants are moved in evacuation cribs.

We practice a disaster drill once a year and keep a written record of each drill for at least one year.

## Field Trips and Transportation



On outings we keep our usual ratios and invite parent volunteers so there is about one adult for every six children. Infants go on outings only with at least one adult for every two babies. Teachers carry each child's signed emergency medical consent form.

For van trips, our drivers hold the correct license for the vehicle and are at least 18 years old. We never exceed the van's seating capacity, keep it in safe working order, and secure every passenger in a seat belt or car seat, with infants in rear-facing infant car seats installed according to the manufacturer's instructions. Ratios are kept in the van, and a first aid kit rides along. If children fall asleep on the drive back, they may finish their nap buckled in the parked van while a teacher checks on them from the office window.



## Health and Safety at the Center



We have no swimming pool or pond, and children never swim while in our care. Water tables are always supervised. Hot water at children's sinks stays between 105 and 120 degrees.

## Child Abuse Reporting

All staff are mandated reporters under California law. Anyone who suspects a child has been abused or neglected must report it right away to the county child protective services agency or the police, and the center also reports to Community Care Licensing.

## Records and Privacy

All information in your child's records is confidential and is shared only as the law allows, such as with Community Care Licensing. You may look at your child's records at any time.

## Questions and Concerns

We share daily notes through our family app and hold conferences twice a year. If you have a concern, please talk with your child's teacher or the director, who will respond within five business days. You may also contact the local licensing office at any time.
