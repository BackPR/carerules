# Mangrove Meadow Learning Center: Family Handbook

Fictional handbook written to test CareRules.

## Welcome

Welcome to Mangrove Meadow Learning Center, a licensed child care center in Palmetto Bend, Florida, serving children from six weeks through twelve years old. We see you as our partner in every part of your child's day. Please keep this handbook handy and ask our director, Ms. Renata Okafor, about anything that is unclear.

## Hours

We are open Monday through Friday, 6:30 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. We close for major holidays and two staff training days, which we announce at least one month ahead.

## Enrollment

Before your child's first day, you will complete the Child Care Application for Enrollment. We cannot accept a child into care until this information is on file. It lists your contact information, emergency contacts, your child's doctor, any allergies or special needs, and the people you authorize to pick up your child.

At enrollment you will receive the Department of Children and Families brochure *Know Your Child Care Facility*, our written discipline and expulsion policies, and our food and nutrition policies, which include our food safety and food allergen practices. You will sign the enrollment form to confirm that you received each of these.

Within 30 days of enrollment, we need a current Florida Certification of Immunization (DH 680) or Religious Exemption from Immunization (DH 681), plus a Student Health Examination (DH 3040), which is valid for two years from the date of the physical. If these forms are not on file within 30 days, your child cannot continue to attend. School-age children are exempt because their school keeps these records. Please be aware that some children in our care may not have current immunizations because of a medical or religious exemption.

## Tuition and Fees

A non-refundable $125 registration fee holds your child's space. Weekly tuition ranges from $150 for school-age children to $285 for infants and is due each Monday, with a $15 late fee after Wednesday. We accept School Readiness and VPK funding. A late pickup fee of $1 per minute applies after 6:00 p.m.

## Arrival and Pickup

Please walk your child into the classroom and sign in with the time on the classroom tablet, then sign out with the time at pickup. Teachers check these entries so our attendance record is always accurate.

We release your child only to you or to the people you have authorized in writing on the enrollment form. Every person picking up your child must show a photo ID, even after we get to know them. To add someone, update your form in writing at the front desk; we cannot accept a phone call or text as authorization.

## Classrooms and Staffing

Children are grouped by age, and we meet Florida's staff-to-child ratios at all times, including on the playground, at meals, and during nap.

| Age of children | Staff-to-child ratio | Our classroom |
|---|---|---|
| Birth to 1 year | 1 : 4 | Minnows |
| 1 year to under 2 years | 1 : 6 | Tadpoles |
| 2 years to under 3 years | 1 : 11 | Sea Turtles |
| 3 years to under 4 years | 1 : 15 | Dolphins |
| 4 years to under 5 years | 1 : 20 | Manatees (VPK) |
| 5 years and older | 1 : 25 | Explorers |

When a group includes any infant under 12 months, one teacher cares for no more than four children of any age. When a group includes a child between 12 and 24 months, one teacher cares for no more than six children. When children two and older are combined, the ratio follows the age group with the most children.

Each teacher is assigned to a specific group and stays with that group. Children are never left without a teacher's supervision, whether inside, on the playground, in a vehicle, or on a field trip. A staff member at least 21 years old is in charge whenever we are open.

## Our Day

Each classroom posts its written lesson plan, which balances active and quiet play, learning centers, meals, and rest. Children play outdoors at least twice a day, weather permitting. We do not use screens with children under two. For children two and older, screens are used only for educational or movement activities and never for more than one hour a day.

## Rest Time and Safe Sleep

After lunch, children in full-day care rest on their own cot or mat, spaced at least 18 inches apart. During rest time, at least one teacher stays within sight and hearing of all resting children, and the other staff needed for ratio remain in the building on the same floor. Children under 24 months are directly supervised at all times, including while they sleep.

Infants nap in their own crib or play yard. Infants who cannot yet roll over are always placed on their backs to sleep on a firm crib mattress with a tight-fitted sheet. Cribs hold nothing else: no bumper pads, pillows, blankets, or stuffed animals, and nothing is placed over the top of a crib. A fitted sleep sack is welcome, but we do not swaddle unless your child's physician authorizes it in writing. Every crib sits at least 18 inches from window blinds and curtains. If an infant falls asleep in a bouncer or a teacher's arms, we move the baby to the crib right away.

## Preventing Shaken Baby Syndrome

Because we care for infants, we follow a written policy and procedure to identify and prevent shaken baby syndrome and abusive head trauma. Staff learn the warning signs, such as extreme irritability, vomiting, poor feeding, or unusual sleepiness, and complete safe sleep training. Teachers learn calming techniques for crying babies and know that, when they feel frustrated, they should lay the baby safely in a crib and ask a coworker to step in. If staff suspect head trauma, they call 911 when needed and notify the director and family.

## Guidance and Discipline Policy

We guide behavior with kindness and consistency. Teachers set simple expectations, redirect children to better choices, help them name their feelings, and praise positive behavior. When a child needs a break, a teacher may use a short calm-down time of about one minute per year of age, where the teacher can see the child. Children are never kept from active play for an entire play period because of misbehavior.

Our discipline is never severe, humiliating, or frightening, and it is never connected to food, rest, or toileting. Spanking and every other kind of physical punishment are prohibited. Staff never hit, pinch, or roughly handle a child, never shame or threaten a child, and never use harsh or sarcastic words. We never isolate a child out of sight, restrain a child, use or withhold food or drinks as a punishment, punish toileting accidents, or place a child in a crib as a time-out.

If behavior concerns continue, we will meet with you to make a written plan together and may suggest outside resources. A child may be dismissed only after we have met with you, tried that plan, and given you two weeks' written notice, unless the child's behavior seriously endangers others and requires immediate removal. We may also end enrollment, with two weeks' written notice, for tuition more than two weeks overdue.

Before your child starts, we give you a written copy of these discipline and expulsion policies, and you sign the enrollment form to confirm you received them. A copy of our current discipline and expulsion policies is always kept at the front desk for parents and the licensing authority to review.

## Meals, Snacks, and Allergies

This section is our food and nutrition policy. We serve breakfast, lunch, and an afternoon snack that follow USDA MyPlate and the Child Care Food Program meal patterns, and we post menus weekly.

To keep food safe, we hold hot foods at 135°F or above and cold foods at 41°F or below. For children under four, we do not serve choking hazards such as whole grapes, hot dog rounds, popcorn, nuts, or cheese cubes. We cut food for infants into pieces a quarter inch or smaller and food for toddlers into pieces a half inch or smaller, including food sent from home.

Please tell us about any food allergy at enrollment. Each child with an allergy needs a written emergency care plan that names the allergen and how to avoid it, gives the treatment steps with the names and doses of any medicines, and lists the symptoms that mean medicine should be given. Allergy lists are posted in the kitchen and each classroom.

## Keeping Children Healthy

Teachers check every child for signs of illness throughout the day. Your child must stay home, or will be sent home, with a temperature of 101°F or higher together with other signs of illness, diarrhea (more than one abnormally loose stool in 24 hours), severe or whooping cough, difficult breathing, a stiff neck, pink eye, open skin sores, or yellowish skin or eyes. A sick child rests in our isolation area, within sight and hearing of a staff member, until you arrive. Your child may return with a note from the doctor or once the symptoms are gone.

A child with head lice may return the following day, once treatment has been done and verified with a product box or a signed note from you. If two or more children show similar symptoms within 72 hours, or a reportable disease is suspected, we notify the county health department immediately and follow its direction.

Our staff follow a written exposure plan based on CDC universal precautions for contact with blood or other body fluids, and every staff member is trained on it before starting work and again every year. We also follow a written cleaning schedule posted in each room: tables, toys, sinks, toilets, diaper pails, and floors are cleaned and sanitized daily, and cribs, cots, mats, linens, and cloth toys are washed weekly.

## Medication

We give medication only with a medication authorization form that you have dated and signed, listing your child's name, the name of the medicine, and the date, time, and amount of each dose. For "as needed" medicines, the form must also describe the symptoms that call for a dose. All medicine must come in its original container, and prescription medicine must have the pharmacy label showing the doctor or pharmacy, your child's name, the medicine, and the directions. Each dose is recorded with your child's full name, the medicine, the date and time, the amount, and the name and signature of the staff member who gave it. Sunscreen, diaper cream, and insect repellent also require your written permission.

## Injuries, Accidents, and Emergencies

We write up every accident or incident on the same day it happens and share the report with you that same day. For a serious illness or injury, we call you right away; for a life-threatening emergency, we call 911 first and then you. Any serious injury is reported immediately to our licensing authority. Emergency numbers, including 911, poison control, the Florida Abuse Hotline, and the county health department, are posted by every phone with our address and directions to the center.

We also keep a written plan for reporting and managing serious incidents, including a lost or missing child, suspected maltreatment, an injury or illness needing emergency treatment, the death of a child or staff member, or a threatening person trying to enter, and for reporting each to the licensing authority.

### Emergency Preparedness and Drills

Our written emergency preparedness plan covers fire, evacuation, relocation, shelter in place, lockdown, and severe weather such as hurricanes and tornadoes. If we must leave the building, children walk to our relocation site at Palmetto Bend Community Hall. The plan explains how we reunite children with families at the center or the relocation site, how we keep you updated by text and phone, and how we meet the needs of children with special needs or medical conditions. Teachers take their class roster and parent contact list during every drill and emergency.

We hold a fire drill every month, at different days and times when children are here, including at least one during nap time and one using an alternate exit route. We also practice a lockdown drill and a severe weather drill at least once each year.

## Field Trips and Transportation

Before every field trip, we post the date, time, and location by the front desk at least two working days ahead, and your child goes only with your signed permission slip. One extra adult beyond the required ratio comes on every trip, and teachers carry emergency contact information, a first aid kit, and a cell phone.

Mangrove Meadow is approved by the Department to provide transportation, and our van has a child safety alarm that reminds the driver to check every seat. For every trip, by van or on foot, we keep a transportation log with each child's name and departure and arrival times, signed by the driver or walking teacher and by a second staff member who confirms every child has arrived safely.

## Reporting Child Abuse and Neglect

Florida law makes every member of our staff a mandatory reporter. Any staff member who suspects that a child has been abused, abandoned, or neglected must report it right away to the Florida Abuse Hotline at 1-800-96-ABUSE. Telling the director does not replace this report.

## Animals at the Center

The Manatees classroom keeps a guinea pig named Clover in a clean, covered cage, and our lobby has a small goldfish aquarium. Our animals are healthy, stay in their cages or tank, and are never kept where food is prepared or served. We will let you know in writing before any new or visiting animal comes to the center.

## A Smoke-Free Center

Smoking of any kind, including cigarettes, e-cigarettes, and vaping, is not allowed anywhere on our premises, including the building, playgrounds, parking lot, and our van.

## Records and Confidentiality

Your child's records are kept confidential and shared only with you, the staff who care for your child, and the licensing authority. All required records are kept here at the center and are available to the licensing authority for review during our hours of operation. Medical records belong to you, and we return them when your child leaves.

## Visiting and Concerns

You are welcome at Mangrove Meadow at any time while we are open. You may visit, observe, or call the center whenever your child is in our care, and no appointment is needed.

If you have a concern, please talk first with your child's teacher, then with Ms. Okafor. You may also contact the Florida Department of Children and Families or the local licensing office at any time.

If ownership of Mangrove Meadow ever changes, we will tell you at least one week before the change by posting a notice at the front entrance, including it in our newsletter, or sending a letter home.

Thank you for trusting us with your child.
