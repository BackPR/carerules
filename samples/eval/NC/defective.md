# Dogwood Lantern Children's Center Family Handbook

Fictional handbook written to test CareRules.

## Welcome

Welcome to Dogwood Lantern Children's Center, a child care center licensed by the North Carolina Division of Child Development and Early Education (DCDEE).

Dogwood Lantern Children's Center, 310 Fictional Laurel Ridge Road, North Carolina. Phone: (919) 555-0183.

## Hours and Days

We are open Monday through Friday, 6:45 a.m. to 6:00 p.m., all year. We close for New Year's Day, Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving Day and the Friday after, and December 24 through 26.

## Ages and Services

We serve children ages 6 weeks through 5 years. We serve breakfast, lunch, and an afternoon snack each day. We do not offer before- or after-school care, and we do not provide transportation. In summer, children 3 and older may join swim lessons at a nearby public pool.

## Enrollment

We encourage both parents, when possible, to tour the center and to visit with your child before the first day. To enroll, you will complete and sign our application, which must be on file by your child's first day. It lists emergency contacts, your child's health care provider, allergies and health needs, fears or behaviors we should know about, and the people allowed to pick up your child. You will also sign a statement authorizing us to get emergency medical care for your child and provide your child's immunization record. Children with allergies, asthma, or other chronic conditions need a medical action plan, updated each year.

During your enrollment conference, on or before your child's first day, we go over this handbook with you and give you a copy. We will notify you in writing of any change in our policies. Every staff member also receives a copy of these policies and any changes.

## Fees and Payment

Weekly tuition is $315 for infants, $290 for toddlers, $270 for twos, and $245 for threes through fives. A $100 registration fee is due at enrollment. Tuition is due each Friday for the following week, by bank draft or card. Payments received after Tuesday carry a $20 late fee, and late pick-up costs $1 per minute after 6:00 p.m.

## What to Bring

- Two complete changes of clothes, labeled
- Diapers and wipes, if your child wears them
- Breast milk, if you choose to provide it (we supply formula)
- A crib-size fitted sheet and small blanket for cot rest (children over 12 months)
- Weather-appropriate outdoor clothes and closed-toe shoes

## Arrival and Pick-Up

Our written arrival and pick-up procedures are posted at the front entrance. Please walk your child to the classroom and sign in and out on the front-desk tablet.

If someone who is not on your enrollment application needs to pick up your child, just call us and we will release your child to that person after checking a photo ID. We will ask for photo ID until we know each person. If a court order affects custody or visitation, please give us a copy; a parent subject to a custody order may visit only as the order allows.

## Classrooms and Staffing

Children are grouped by age: Infants (6 weeks to 12 months), Toddlers (12 to 24 months), Twos, Threes, and Fours and Fives. We meet the North Carolina staff/child ratios, and the ratio for each classroom is posted there:

| Age of children | Staff to children | Maximum group size |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 12 months | 1:5 | 10 |
| 12 to 24 months | 1:6 | 12 |
| 2 to 3 years | 1:10 | 20 |
| 3 to 4 years | 1:15 | 25 |
| 4 to 5 years | 1:20 | 25 |
| 5 years and older | 1:25 | 25 |

During the first and last hour of the day, groups may be combined, always at the ratio for the youngest child in the group.

## Daily Schedule

Each classroom posts its schedule and activity plan. A typical day for Threes, Fours, and Fives:

- 6:45 Arrival and free-choice play
- 7:30 Breakfast
- 8:15 Morning meeting
- 8:45 Learning centers: art, books, blocks, manipulatives, and dramatic play
- 9:45 Outdoor play
- 10:45 Teacher-led small groups and music
- 11:15 Lunch
- 12:00 Rest time
- 2:30 Afternoon snack
- 3:00 Outdoor play
- 4:00 Free-choice play until pick-up

Infants and toddlers follow their own routines for eating, napping, and diapering. Weather permitting, children go outside every day: at least 30 minutes for children under 2 and at least 60 minutes for children 2 and older.

Toddlers and twos may watch a short educational video, about 20 minutes, after lunch. For children 3 and older, screen time is rare, limited to 30 minutes a day and 2.5 hours a week, tied to a learning goal, and recorded in the activity plan.

## Infant Safe Sleep

Our written safe sleep policy is posted in the infant room. We give and explain a copy to you on or before your baby's first day, and you sign an acknowledgement that we keep in your baby's file.

- Babies 12 months and younger sleep on their backs unless we have a written waiver from a health care professional, or, for babies older than 6 months, from a health care professional or parent.
- Each baby sleeps alone in a crib with a firm mattress and fitted sheet. Nothing else goes in the crib: no pillows, blankets, bumper pads, toys, positioners, or loose bedding.
- We do not swaddle, never cover a baby's head or face, and do not use pacifiers that clip to clothing.
- The infant room stays at 75°F or cooler while babies sleep.
- A teacher looks in person at each sleeping baby at least every 15 minutes and initials the sleep chart.
- A baby who dozes off in a swing or car seat may finish the nap there as long as a teacher can see her.

If we amend our safe sleep policy, we will give infant families written notice at least 14 days before the change takes effect and ask you to sign an acknowledgement.

## Preventing Shaken Baby Syndrome

Our policy on preventing shaken baby syndrome and abusive head trauma is given and explained to you on or before your child's first day, and you sign an acknowledgement. Staff learn to recognize and report warning signs such as irritability, trouble breathing or staying awake, seizures, vomiting, and bruises, and a teacher who feels frustrated by a crying child may ask a coworker to step in. Shaking, tossing, or pushing a child is strictly prohibited. If we change this policy, we will give you written notice 14 days before the new policy takes effect and ask you to sign that you received it.

## Guidance and Discipline

Our teachers listen to children, praise effort, and help them solve problems. We guide behavior with clear expectations, redirection, and choices that fit each child's age and development.

Staff never spank, shake, push, pinch, or handle a child roughly. We never place a child in a locked room or leave a child alone in a room away from staff. We never let another child discipline a child, withhold food or use food as a reward, discipline children for toileting accidents or for not sleeping at rest time, or assign cleaning chores as punishment. Physical activity is never withheld or required as punishment. Children are never yelled at, shamed, frightened, or threatened.

We give you a written copy of this discipline policy and explain it at enrollment, and you sign a statement that we discussed it. If we change it, we will give you written notice 14 days before the change and ask you to sign that we discussed it.

## Meals and Nutrition

Our meals and snacks follow the USDA meal patterns for child care, and children are offered a meal or snack at least every four hours. The current week's dated menu is posted by the kitchen and the front desk. We serve water, breast milk or formula, unflavored whole milk for children 12 to 23 months, unflavored low-fat or skim milk for children 2 and older, and up to 12 ounces of 100% fruit juice per day. Water is always available.

Food allergies and special diets are posted in the kitchen and in your child's eating area. For each child under 15 months, you or your child's health care provider will give us a written feeding plan, which we follow and update as your child grows. Mothers may nurse or pump in our private family room.



## Illness and Mildly Ill Children

Dogwood Lantern provides care for mildly ill children. A child older than 2 months with a temperature below 101°F, or a baby younger than 2 months with a temperature below 100.4°F, may attend unless the child has:

- More than two stools above normal, or diarrhea not contained by a diaper or causing accidents
- Two or more episodes of vomiting within 12 hours
- Lice (until the first treatment), scabies, or chicken pox or a rash that looks like it
- Strep throat (until 12 hours after starting antibiotics and fever-free), impetigo (until 24 hours after starting treatment), pertussis (until five days after starting treatment), hepatitis A, or tuberculosis
- A health professional's written order to stay apart from other children
- Symptoms that keep the child from participating comfortably, or that need more care than staff can safely give

A child who becomes ill at the center rests apart from the other children until picked up. We will call you if your mildly ill child gets worse.

## Medication

We give prescription and over-the-counter medicine, and apply sunscreen, diaper cream, or insect repellent, only with your written authorization and written instructions. Prescription medicine must be in its original container with the pharmacy label. Over-the-counter medicine must be in its original package labeled with your child's name; permission lasts up to 30 days and never exceeds the label dose. Written standing authorizations are available for asthma or allergy medicine (up to six months) and for sunscreen and creams (up to 12 months). Each dose is recorded with your child's name, the date, time, amount, and type of medicine, and the name and signature of the staff member who gave it. If a medication error happens, we will call 911 as our training directs, notify the director, call you, watch your child closely, and document the error.

## Injuries and Medical Emergencies

Our director, or the assistant director as alternate, carries out our emergency medical care plan. For serious injuries or illness, we call 911, and a staff member brings your signed emergency medical authorization and goes with your child while another calls you. Each time a child is injured, we complete an incident report that you sign and receive a copy of. If the injury needs medical treatment, we mail a copy to DCDEE within seven days.

## Emergency Preparedness and Drills

Our written Emergency Preparedness and Response Plan covers fire, severe weather, floods, power outages, chemical spills, and a dangerous person nearby. It explains how staff account for every child, move infants in wheeled evacuation cribs, help children with special needs, relocate to our alternate site, and reunite you with your child. All staff review the plan at orientation and every year.

Fire drills are held twice a year, in the spring and fall, and we keep a record of each one. Shelter-in-place or lockdown drills are held at least every three months, and evacuation cribs are used during every drill.

## Walks, Outings, and Swimming

Children take walks around the neighborhood and to Fictional Maple Park. Before staff walk your child off our property, we need your written permission; you may give standing permission, good for up to 12 months, for regular walks.

A current schedule of outings is posted in each participating classroom, and you receive a copy. It shows the date, time, location, and purpose of each outing and the name of the person to contact in an emergency.

Swim lessons are open to children 3 and older with your written permission, which includes a statement that you have read our aquatic policies. Lifeguards are on duty, and we keep ratios of 1:8 for 3-year-olds, 1:10 for 4-year-olds, and 1:13 for children 5 and older, with at least two staff members supervising at all times.

## Safety at the Center

Our playground is fenced, checked every morning, and inspected monthly by a staff member trained in playground safety. Hazardous items are kept locked away, and firearms are not allowed except when carried by a law enforcement officer.





## Reporting Child Abuse and Neglect

Any staff member who suspects that a child has been abused or neglected reports it right away to the Department of Social Services in the county where the child lives, and then tells the director. Staff do not need anyone's permission to report. If a concern involves a member of our staff, the director also notifies DCDEE, and that person is kept away from children during the investigation. All staff are trained to recognize and respond to suspected child maltreatment.

## Records

Your child's application and medical information are kept where caregiving staff can reach them while your child is in care, and we update emergency information whenever it changes and at least once a year. Records are kept while your child is enrolled and for one year after.

## Family Involvement

You are welcome to visit any time we are open; just let the administrator on site know when you arrive. Our family involvement plan, posted in the lobby, includes daily conversations with teachers, family conferences each fall and spring, and chances to take part individually or as a group: reading to a class, joining family picnics and workdays, or serving on our Family Advisory Committee.

## Questions and Concerns

If you need information or have a concern, please talk first with your child's teacher. If the issue is not resolved, contact the director, who will meet with you within two business days and follow up in writing. You may also contact DCDEE at 1-800-859-0829 at any time.
