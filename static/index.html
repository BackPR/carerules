Your handbook is a signed promise.

State rules require centers to write their policies down and give them to families. In Texas, for example, parents sign them when they enroll. So if your handbook says something the rules don't allow, it's in writing, often with a parent's signature on it.

1

Write your policies down.

You must develop written operational policies and procedures

Hours, pickup, illness, medicine, emergencies and more.

Texas §746.501, page 32
2

Parents sign them.

On or before the date of admission, the parents must sign a child-care enrollment agreement or other similar documents, which must include at least the operational policies

Your policies are part of what every family signs.

Texas §746.503, page 37
3

Changes go in writing too.

At least one copy of the updated operational policies or child-care enrollment agreement must be signed and dated for each family

You must also tell parents about changes in writing.

Texas §746.505, page 37

The rules usually say "written policies." Most centers call them a parent handbook or family handbook. Same thing. Michigan's rules use the word "handbook": a center "shall provide a handbook, electronically or hard copy, to each parent enrolling a child" (R 400.8113, page 11 of the PDF download). Many states have a rule like this. CareRules checks your handbook against your state's rules.

Built on the official rules.

CareRules uses Beam 1.0, an AI model from Reflection that can read very long documents. It works from the official rules of 22 states and DC, about 3,000 pages, downloaded from each state's website.

1

It lists what's required.

Beam has read each state's full rulebook and listed what a handbook must cover. When you check yours, it compares your handbook with that list and the matching rule pages.

2

It checks every quote.

Each finding quotes your handbook and the rule. Then a simple text match (no AI) checks that both quotes appear in the original documents, word for word. If one doesn't, the finding says "check by hand."

3

You get the fixes.

A score for each state, what breaks a rule, what's missing or incomplete, and new wording to paste in. Print the report or download the wording.

    Questions

    What's a parent handbook?

    It's your center's written policies for families, given when they enroll: hours, pickup, illness, medicine, meals, discipline, emergencies and more. State rules require them. In Texas, for example, centers must have written policies (§746.501), and parents sign them on or before the first day (§746.503).

    Is this legal advice?

    No. CareRules shows you the rule's own words and its page so you can check them yourself. Confirm any change with your licensing agency.

    Which states does it cover?

    22 states and DC: Alabama, Arkansas (out-of-school-time programs only), California, Colorado, District of Columbia, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Iowa, Michigan, Mississippi, Missouri, New Jersey, New Mexico, North Carolina, North Dakota, Oklahoma, Oregon, Pennsylvania, South Carolina, Texas, Virginia, and Washington State. Not listed? Email hi@backpr.com to ask about your state.

    What happens to my handbook?

    Your file is read in your browser and only its text is sent. It goes through our checking service and Hugging Face to Beam (made by Reflection, hosted by Baseten). Hugging Face says it doesn't store requests or train on them; Baseten says it doesn't store inputs or outputs. We keep Beam's answer, which quotes your handbook, for 7 days, then delete it. We don't keep your file. You can remove staff phone numbers first; the check doesn't need them.

    Privacy: who sees my handbook?

    CareRules is run by BackPR (hi@backpr.com). Your file is read in your browser; only its text is sent, through our checking service on Cloudflare and Hugging Face to Beam, hosted by Baseten. We keep Beam's answer, which quotes your handbook, for 7 days so a repeat check is free, then delete it. To limit no-code sample checks, we keep a one-way scrambled form of your internet address for about two days. Your access code is remembered in your browser. We use no cookies, ads or analytics, and we use your email only to send your code unless you ask us for something else. To ask about or delete your data, email hi@backpr.com.

    Can it read a scanned handbook?

    No. It needs a file with real text, like the Word version or a PDF saved from Word or Google Docs. Right after you choose a file, the page tells you how many pages it could read.

    Who decided what's required?

    Beam read each state's full licensing rules and listed what a center's handbook must cover. Every item quotes the rule, and a computer check confirmed each quote word for word. A lawyer hasn't reviewed the lists, so treat them as a starting point.

    Does it cover family child care homes?

    No. It's built for licensed child care centers. Most states license family child care homes under separate rules, which CareRules doesn't check yet.

    Every finding shows your handbook's words and the rule's words, with a link to the rule's page, so you can check it. If one is wrong, email hi@backpr.com; it helps us improve.

    How accurate is it?

Real handbooks are harder to check, so read every finding. A quote that matches word for word proves the words are really there, not that Beam understood them correctly.

    What does the score mean?

    It's the percent of required policies your handbook covers (incomplete ones count half), minus 5 points for each sentence that breaks a rule. It's not an inspection result.
